The answer is: orientation IS sent in the standard protocol, just not explicitly. Look at section III.5 again — the welcome response doesn't include orientation. But the subject says the player "is oriented randomly" when an egg hatches. For the initial connection it's also random. So the original protocol assumes the client figures out its orientation through gameplay, not through a handshake field.
The practical implication is that a naive client genuinely cannot know its starting orientation. The classic Zappy approach to handle this is:
The client sends voir immediately on connection. The vision response tells you what's around you in local coordinates — forward, left, right. From that you can't directly read your orientation, but you can start dead-reckoning from whatever your first turn command produces. The client just accepts "I don't know where I face" until it has moved at least once.
Your server extension that sends orientation in the welcome message is actually the correct thing to do for a well-behaved implementation. The original protocol is deficient here — there's no good reason to hide the spawn orientation from the client. Your partner's concern about "not allowed" is misreading the constraint, which is about client-to-client communication, not server-to-client data.
So to directly answer your question: without the orientation field in the welcome message, the first broadcast direction received after spawning would be interpreted against whatever default orientation the client assumes (North in your case), which would only be correct by luck. Your extension fixes a genuine protocol gap. Keep it, it's correct.
